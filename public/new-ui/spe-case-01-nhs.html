Discovery

What the research found

Fourteen interviews, nine prototype sessions and one technical spike, over eight weeks.

Information governance passes, but on conditions

Three of four IG leads would approve a cloud processor — with UK data residency and a privacy impact assessment signed before any patient data moves. One trust made an on-premise option a hard condition, so it entered the architecture as a later-phase commitment rather than a surprise.

Gate 1 · IG

Alert fatigue comes from true positives, not false ones

Quality Managers did not reject wrong alerts. They rejected too many right ones. A list showing every ward above threshold is unusable, so the default view became the top three deteriorating wards, with everything else one toggle away.

Gate 2 · Alert design

Ward managers will not log in — ever

All four said so unprompted, before being asked. That single finding moved the ward-facing dashboard out of scope and made the Monday email the primary touchpoint, with a no-password link that expires after seven days.

Gate 2 · Adoption

The near-term driver is compliance, not payment

The 10 Year Health Plan’s patient power payment is still in trial. The Medium Term Planning Framework already mandates near-real-time feedback. Compliance leads the conversation; the payment linkage follows it.

Gate 3 · Commercial

Deferred on the record rather than dropped: the closed-loop action log, the portfolio view linking experience to outcomes, and integration with Datix, PALS and the NHS App.

Phase 01 of 06

Strategy

In the framework

Frame the opportunity and establish whether the data to serve it legally exists.

The phase that reshaped the product. What looked like a national data play turned out to be an integration play — established before a single adapter was scoped.

What this phase delivers

Synthesis of NHS policy, FFT publications and vendor landscape
What we deliverHow S&PE differentiates

A dated map of adjacent vendors and the policy drivers behind trust income, read across the FFT publication set and the 10 Year Health Plan. It set the target: patient experience, not complaints handling. Where to play was a human call, taken on the record before scoping.

The crowded end of this market is complaints handling. The scan found where trust income is actually moving under the 10 Year Health Plan and pointed the product at the gap — a positioning call made from sourced evidence in phase 01, not after a failed pilot.

A written finding on the national FFT dataset: monthly aggregate counts, no free text, five to six weeks behind month end. The answer to "can we get the data" arrived before scoping, in writing. Narrowing the first market to per-trust integrations was decided by people, in writing, before build.

This finding would have surfaced mid-build on most programmes and taken the business case with it. Because it landed at phase 01, the product became a per-trust integration by design and the first market was narrowed deliberately rather than discovered to be impossible.

A north star — feedback themes acted on per trust, per month — with four input drivers behind it. The metric was chosen by the team, not derived by a model.

Every vendor in this space reports sentiment score. We committed to coverage as the leading indicator, because a score nobody acts on is not an outcome. The metric then held the delivery team to account at every later gate.

Produced: competitor and policy map · ingestion build brief · north star framework · written national-data position.

Phase 02 of 06 · The engine

Discovery

In the framework

Generate, test and kill hypotheses continuously — and keep running underneath the phases that follow.

Three clinical personas across three layers, a nine-lane service blueprint, and the finding that killed a whole surface before it was designed.

What this phase delivers

Persona simulation across three clinical roles · blueprint generation
What we deliverHow S&PE differentiates

Chief Nurse, Quality Manager and Ward Manager personas built from published NHS role evidence, with goals, pains and motivators, plus simulated review transcripts. Reading Ward Manager adoption as the gating risk was a human judgement on simulated evidence, not a model output.

It identified the Ward Manager — not the Chief Nurse — as the make-or-break persona. A dashboard-first product would have served the budget holder and been ignored by the person who has twelve-hour shifts and three minutes. Finding that before build is the difference between adoption and shelfware.

A service blueprint across six journey stages and nine swimlanes, every AI-augmented step marked, both lines of visibility drawn.

It fixed, explicitly, where AI ranks and where a human always reads the comment. In a clinical setting that boundary is the governance conversation — and we brought it as a drawn document rather than a reassurance.

A risk-ranked hypothesis log with the cheapest test named against each assumption. Every deferral was a human call and is recorded as one.

It split MVP from post-MVP on evidence: the closed-loop action log and the portfolio view were deferred, deliberately and on the record, rather than promised and dropped.

Produced: three-persona pack · six-stage service blueprint · hypothesis log with MVP boundary.

Phase 03 of 06

Design

In the framework

Every iteration is an experiment, built on the client's own design system with accessibility as a floor.

Five dashboard iterations and an email-first digest, built on NHS design system tokens with WCAG 2.1 AA written into the component work rather than audited afterwards.

What this phase delivers

Prototype generation on NHS design system v0.176.0 · accessibility critique
What we deliverHow S&PE differentiates

Five dashboard iterations built on real NHS design tokens, components and type — working prototypes that survive an NHS design review rather than slideware. Which iteration went in front of users was decided at each round by a person.

The default view became top-three deteriorating wards, because research showed a full alert list produces alert fatigue and gets ignored. That is a design decision taken from user evidence and reversible in a toggle, not a preference argued in a workshop.

WCAG 2.1 AA specified per component — contrast, focus order, keyboard paths, reduced motion — with print views on every surface. AA conformance was signed off per component by a person, not certified by a tool.

Accessibility was treated as a build floor, not a launch-gate exercise. For an NHS-facing tool that is the difference between passing assessment and carrying a public finding.

Digest and dashboard copy written to NHS sentence-case conventions, ward-facing content readable in under three minutes.

Patient words are always shown verbatim and never paraphrased by the model. Where a patient wrote it, a human reads it — which is what makes clinical staff trust what the tool shows them.

Produced: dashboard v1 to v5 · ward digest email · magic-link destination page · service start page · design decision log.

Phase 04 of 06

Delivery

In the framework

Build the agreed hypothesis and nothing that arrived without evidence.

Decoupled microsites behind a React and Firebase host, with the decision log wired into CI so the promote script fails when an architectural choice goes unrecorded.

What this phase delivers

Code generation from reviewed prompts · decision capture · self-review
What we deliverHow S&PE differentiates

A working microsite: scaffold, components, pipeline and Playwright specs per surface, generated from reviewed build prompts.

The microsite boundary was set by a human and held: one route, one nav link, no cross-imports, its own data layer. That is why the NHS surface can be governed, assessed and deployed on its own terms rather than entangled with the host showcase.

Deviations flagged against the architecture and design decision logs before promotion, not found in review.

The build reviewed itself against the recorded intent, and a human ruled on whether each deviation was a decision or a defect. Nothing drifted quietly.

Architecture and design decision entries written as the work happened, enforced by CI as a promotion gate. Every entry was written and approved by a person; CI only enforces that it exists.

The pipeline will not promote an undocumented architectural change. Months later, "why is it built this way" has a written answer — which is what makes the codebase handed over rather than inherited.

Produced: decoupled microsite scaffold · namespaced configuration · enforced decision log · generated components with tests.

Flows straight into Validation & QA — in the source lifecycle, build and test sit inside one combined Delivery stage, gated only once testing is complete.

Phase 05 of 06

Validation & QA

In the framework

Confirmation, not discovery — every claim carries a measurement.

Two rounds of persona-simulated research before any real NHS user saw the build, and one accuracy claim withdrawn rather than rounded up.

What this phase delivers

Persona-simulated research rounds · Playwright generation · model evaluation
What we deliverHow S&PE differentiates

Two research rounds with all three personas scored against their own goals. Round 1 found two demo-blocking failures; round 2 confirmed both fixed. Which failures blocked the demo was a human call.

It also named what was still unresolved: the ward digest, not the dashboard, was the remaining risk. We report the open failure rather than the closed one, because the alternative is discovering it in front of the client.

A Playwright acceptance suite generated from the written criteria and run at three viewports, mapped to each of the eight research fixes. The criteria were agreed by people before any test was generated.

"Done" was defined per fix before the test was written, so the suite proves the criteria rather than the happy path — and it keeps running after we leave.

Theme classification measured against the labelled data available: 83.4% observed accuracy, methodology stated beside it. Withdrawing our own number was a human decision, taken against our own interest.

The 91% figure was withdrawn from all client-facing material the moment the measurement did not support it. We would rather show a smaller number we can defend than a larger one a client's data team can dismantle.

Produced: two round-by-round research reports · Playwright acceptance suite · accessibility evidence · a stated model-quality position.

Phase 06 of 06 · Loops back

Operations & product monitoring

In the framework

AI becomes part of the product, and the product produces the next round of hypotheses.

The platform reports on itself: product metrics, system health, AI quality, compliance coverage and an archive of every signal it has raised.

What this phase delivers

Embedded CQC-domain classification · natural-language analytics · benchmark anomaly detection
What we deliverHow S&PE differentiates

Every comment classified against the regulator's quality domains and severity-scored — a pre-ranked ward and theme queue ready before anyone logs in. What the service works on each week stays a ward decision.

Feedback here is normally sampled at ten to twenty percent and coded by hand over days. The queue is built against the regulator's own domains, so the same work that runs the week also builds the inspection evidence.

Plain-English answers over the platform's own performance data, with the caveat attached, no analyst in the loop. A person still decides what reaches the board pack.

The caveat travels with the number. A trend cannot be lifted into a board pack without the qualification that makes it honest.

Benchmark comparison against national FFT with anomalies flagged and routed into the hypothesis log.

A human still rules on whether an anomaly is real signal or a volume artefact — and when it is real, it re-enters discovery as a hypothesis. The lifecycle loops instead of ending at go-live.

Produced: performance analytics microsite · AI signals archive · model cost, latency and quality tracked as product metrics.

Phase 03 · Jul 2026

Who it is for

Strategic

Chief Nurse

Trust-wide risk before the board asks, a board pack without three days of manual compilation, and a regulator evidence pack in one click.

What they need

Which services are at risk before the board asks, CQC Well-Led evidence on demand, and a red alert within 24 hours of a service crossing its threshold.

What blocks them today

Ward problems surfacing through the press or a complaint rather than the data. Board packs costing the quality team two to three days every quarter. CQC evidence taking weeks to assemble.

Analytical

Quality Manager

Every comment classified rather than a sample, top-three deteriorating wards by default, alert to theme to verbatim in three clicks.

What they need

Every comment processed rather than a tenth of them, quarterly FFT coding down from days to under two hours, and a closed loop: log an action, watch whether the score recovers.

What blocks them today

Coding FFT free text by hand in Excel. Dashboards that flag every ward above threshold, so nothing can be prioritised. No way to show the board that feedback led to a measurable improvement.

Operational

Ward Manager

A Monday 08:00 email. No login, under three minutes, always at least one positive comment to take to the team.

What they need

Last week’s feedback in one email, readable in under three minutes on a twelve-hour shift, and one positive comment worth reading out at the Monday huddle.

What blocks them today

Feedback arriving four to six weeks after the stay, too late to fix anything or thank anyone. Aggregate scores that say nothing specific. Positive comments never reaching the ward — only complaints do.

Limits

What this case does not claim

Stated here rather than left for someone to find.

Three gates, in order

Comment-level intelligence sits behind a per-trust data-access agreement, then a privacy impact assessment signed by the trust's data protection officer, then a completed security toolkit with its penetration test. Nothing touches identifiable data before those clear, which is why operational confidence stops at supported.

Open items we are not hiding

Theme-classification accuracy is 83.4% observed against a 91% target, and no labelled validation dataset exists yet. The alert engine needs volume normalisation and service-type benchmarking before it is reliable against real data. The analytics prototype needs a server-side model proxy before publication.

And what it does establish

Problem and desirability confidence are evidenced across all three personas, and technical feasibility is evidenced for the frontend layer, which is green-lit for productionisation. Two ideas were eliminated in the cheapest phases of the lifecycle.
